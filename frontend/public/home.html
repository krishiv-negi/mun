Model United Nations (MUN)

An academic simulation of the United Nations where students step into the role of global diplomats to debate international crises and negotiate real-world solutions.

Understanding the Conference

In a Model United Nations (MUN) conference, participating students—called delegates—are assigned a specific country or role (a portfolio) inside a simulated UN body such as the General Assembly (UNGA), Security Council (UNSC), or Human Rights Council (UNHRC). Delegates research their country's foreign policy, deliver speeches, build alliances during caucuses, and co-author formal resolutions to address pressing global challenges while developing leadership, public speaking, and diplomatic negotiation skills.

Committees & Agendas

Each committee simulates a real UN council focused on a specific international agenda, from peacekeeping to climate policy.

Country Portfolios

Delegates represent an assigned nation's official stance rather than personal opinions, mastering global geopolitics and research.

Debate & Caucuses

Sessions combine formal speeches, moderated topic debates, and unmoderated lobbying to draft consensus-driven working papers.

Resolutions & Recognition

Committees vote on final draft resolutions, and outstanding delegates earn awards such as Best Delegate and High Commendation.

Key Terms Used in This Portal

Delegate A registered student participant representing a country or portfolio in a committee.
Committee A simulated UN council (e.g., UNGA, UNSC) with its own seat capacity and minimum MUN requirement.
Portfolio The specific country or seat allocated to a delegate inside a committee.
MUN Eligibility Score Your verified past MUN count extracted from your CV, which determines which committee tiers you can request.
